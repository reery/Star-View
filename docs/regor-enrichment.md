# Regor component and orbit review

Reviewed 2026-10-15. Frozen adoption: `catalog-work/star-systems/reviewed-regor.json`.

The old effective system exposed A, Ba and Bb. The MSC's uncertain Aab–Ac relationship disconnected the confirmed 78.5-day Aa–Ab spectroscopic pair from the outer hierarchy. Its two wide relationships also share parent label `t`, causing one to overwrite the other during automatic traversal. The reviewed branch selection restores Aa, Ab, Ba, Bb and C without accepting uncertain Ac or optical D/E membership. Existing component IDs remain stable; `bright-regor` and `hip-39953` continue to identify the WC8 star, now labelled Aa under the frozen MSC convention. The newly represented O star is Ab. Papers frequently use WR/O instead of these hierarchical labels; no mass or orbit argument is assigned by brightness ordering.

The primary source is [Schmutz et al. 2026, arXiv:2606.27265v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27265v1), a **preprint**, Table 4 and Sections 6.3–6.4. Aa receives WR mass 8.3 ± 0.7 M☉, model radius R*=3.4 ± 0.5 R☉ and bolometric luminosity 110000 ± 40000 L☉. Ab receives O-star mass 26.4 ± 0.5 M☉, radius 13.5 ± 1.0 R☉, effective temperature 35000 ± 300 K and bolometric luminosity 250000 ± 40000 L☉. The O star's 6.9 Myr age is a rotational evolutionary-track estimate without a quoted uncertainty, not an age measurement for either the WR star or the whole system.

The WR atmosphere temperature T*=57100 ± 1700 K belongs to the model's inner boundary. It is retained and displayed as a scoped observation, with `temperature_k` left null so ordinary effective-temperature, blackbody and H–R calculations cannot consume it. The adopted R* follows the optical model radius ratio; the discrepant infrared continuum radius and much larger interferometric emitting-region sizes are not used as stellar radii. The original unresolved catalog photometry, physical estimates, astrometry, errors and provenance remain in the component's retained catalog sources. MSC A's original V=1.78 and derived absolute V are explicitly retained in the review/audit, and are no longer treated as individual WR photometry. No individual Johnson V is inferred for Aa or Ab.

C is the exact MSC `Cphr` companion and SIMBAD `CD-46 3848` / `WDS J08095-4720C`, with Gaia DR3 `5519219999721187968`. The existing frozen, component-specific SIMBAD and Gaia inputs supply its B6V spectrum, Johnson V=7.29 and model physical properties. Their uncertainties and model status remain in `catalog-companions-audit.json`; the existing catalog distance convention is retained.

The Aa–Ab relative orbit adopts the preprint's coherent Table 4 solution: P=78.524 ± 0.002 days, a=1.170 ± 0.004 AU, e=0.322 ± 0.004, i=65.0 ± 0.3°, Ω=247.0 ± 0.2°, ω_WR=67.5 ± 0.4° and T0=MJD 60250.44 ± 0.07. The AU axis uses the paper's assumed cluster distance 336 ± 1 pc, rather than being rescaled using the legacy map parallax. The WR argument refers to primary Aa; the projection converts it by 180° for secondary Ab and uses the equivalent node at 67° to reconcile the source component convention. The reported node at 247° remains displayed. Signed GRAVITY RA/Dec positions in Table 1 independently verify this normalization; omitting it reverses the stars. The orbit source link explicitly identifies the preprint. The superseded 1990 MSC spectroscopic solution remains in the orbit audit. Ba–Bb's 1.4826-day spectroscopic solution is preserved without an axis or sky orientation. No orbit is inferred from estimated wide periods or angular separations.

Regenerate with `python3 scripts/author-stellar-companions.py --write`, then `python3 scripts/author-stellar-orbits.py`. Run `npm run catalog:preservation` and the focused Regor/component/orbit checks. All source CSV catalogs and their provenance remain intact; the shared effective component pool supplies the same reviewed data to Bright, Western and embedded Vela selections.
