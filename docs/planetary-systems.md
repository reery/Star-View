# Planetary systems

The first reviewed planet entry is the Solar System. `src/data/planetary-systems.json` associates its eight planets with the stable `sun` catalog ID. An absent entry means the app has no reviewed planet data for that host; it does not assert that the star has no known planets.

Orbital distances are heliocentric semi-major axes from [JPL Solar System Dynamics, Keplerian elements Table 1](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/approx_pos.html), at J2000.0, rounded to two decimal places in AU for display. Earth's source entry is the Earth–Moon barycenter; this distinction is below the displayed precision. Values describe orbital size, not instantaneous distance or a simulated planet position.

The System tab uses logarithmic spacing between planet markers so the inner planets remain distinguishable beside Neptune. The Sun sits at a symbolic origin to the left. Orbit ellipses, colors, body sizes, and marker alignment are schematic. Scale details remain in the diagram's accessible description; the visible strip contains the planets and Sun label. Selecting a planet row highlights its marker and orbit; Mercury, Venus and Earth also open description cards beside the selected-object card.

The selected-star preview includes a decorative orbital ring and planet dot only for reviewed planet hosts. Stellar component identity and direct component selection remain handled by `star-systems.ts`.

## Mercury specifications

`src/data/planet-details.json` stores reviewed Mercury values and the matching Earth references from the [NASA Mercury fact sheet](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/mercuryfact.html) and [Earth fact sheet](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html). Definitions and sources are available in the specification rows' hover details. The card reuses the selected-object property layout and subgroup headers. Specs and Info are populated; Orbit remains disabled, and the Moons control is hidden for Mercury's zero moon count.

`planet-properties.ts` derives mean diameter and the spherical surface-area approximation from mean radius and calculates comparisons against the same Earth quantity. The solar day is 4,222.6 hours (175.94 days), not the 115.88-day orbital synodic period. Albedo is Bond albedo; Temperature is equivalent black-body equilibrium temperature. Approximate nighttime/daytime limits come from [NASA Mercury Facts](https://science.nasa.gov/mercury/facts/) (−180/430 °C, rounded to 93/703 K); their ratios use Earth's mean surface temperature, 288 K.

Planetary absolute V magnitude uses 1 AU from Sun and observer at zero phase angle; its Earth comparison is a difference in magnitudes. Apparent magnitude is the sourced brightest value from Earth, explicitly labeled rather than presented as a current ephemeris. There is no Earth ratio for apparent magnitude observed from Earth.

Surface pressure is an approximate upper limit: 5 × 10⁻¹⁵ bar converts to 5 × 10⁻¹⁰ Pa. Its comparison preserves the upper-limit qualification. Mercury's surface-bounded exosphere is identified in hover details, with important detected atoms listed without invented abundance percentages.

The lock preserves the planet card during scene interactions and changes of selected host. Escape and reopening the active Mercury row close it; other physical cards retain the established single-detail-card behavior. Section buttons sit beside the planet name and reuse the bordered lock-button style. Values align to the left in a compact second column.

## Mercury information

`src/planet-info.ts` contains original, concise prose reviewed on October 7, 2026, with an introduction and the six requested sections. Images keep their full frames and native aspect ratios. Each caption links to NASA's original image entry; full institutional credits, acquisition dates and color context are available on hover and to assistive technology. Scientific section links and linked mission/history labels provide compact access to primary sources. Switching between Specs and Info resets the card's scroll position.

Sources for the introductory, surface, magnetic and orbital facts are [NASA Mercury Facts](https://science.nasa.gov/mercury/facts/), [NASA's geological findings](https://www.nasa.gov/solar-system/messenger-provides-new-look-at-mercurys-surprising-core-and-landscape-curiosities/), [NASA's hollows overview](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/planets/mercury/mercurys-strange-hollows/), and the [MESSENGER mission](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/messenger/). The core description distinguishes a modeled solid inner core from direct observation and uses [NASA's gravity/rotation analysis](https://www.nasa.gov/solar-system/a-closer-look-at-mercurys-spin-and-gravity-reveals-the-planets-inner-solid-core/); the 85% figure refers to radius, not volume.

Probe coverage uses the [Mariner 10](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mariner-10/) and MESSENGER mission records. BepiColombo's November 2026 orbit insertion is a **plan**, verified against [ESA arrival updates](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/BepiColombo/Latest_updates_BepiColombo_s_arrival_at_Mercury) on October 7, 2026; review that sentence after insertion or a schedule change. Historical sources are [NASA's exploration overview](https://science.nasa.gov/mercury/exploration/), [ESA's account of Gassendi's 1631 transit observation](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/SMART-1/Gassendi_crater_-_clue_on_the_thermal_history_of_Mare_Humorum), and [McGovern et al. (1965), NASA GISS](https://www.giss.nasa.gov/pubs/abs/mc04000r.html), supported by [Pettengill and Dyce's original Arecibo report](https://doi.org/10.1038/2061240a0).

Image files, credits, original URLs and reuse terms are documented in `src/assets/planets/README.md`.

## Mercury globe and temperature conversions

Specs opens with a 172 px black strip containing a rotatable Three.js sphere. `src/planet-globe.ts` maps an actual MESSENGER global monochrome mosaic to the sphere. Upper-left illumination leaves a narrow lower-right night-side sliver, with faint ambient fill, a subtle specular highlight and restrained bloom on the brightest surface areas. This is an illustrative view, without a simulated orbital phase, inferred terrain heights, or atmosphere. Dragging supports mouse, pen and touch; arrow keys also rotate it. There are no visible labels or controls in the strip. Rendering is requested only for texture loading, rotation, resizing or redisplaying the canvas; no idle animation loop runs. Its renderer, postprocessing resources, texture, geometry, event handlers and resize observer are disposed during hot reload.

The two Kelvin values use the same styled, keyboard-accessible tooltip as the Mass card metrics. Celsius = K − 273.15; Fahrenheit = (K − 273.15) × 9/5 + 32. The equilibrium temperature retains one decimal place; the approximate integer-K surface limits use integer conversions. Kelvin comparisons against Earth stay in the property row. Quantity definitions remain on the label's hover details and in the value button's accessible label.

## Venus specifications and information

Venus uses the same dock card, section buttons, lock, scroll container, Earth comparisons and temperature conversions as Mercury. Specs and Info are populated; Orbit remains disabled and the zero-moon Moons control is hidden. Opening Venus from the Sun's System tab resets to Specs. Switching between Venus and Mercury retains one WebGL renderer, replaces the texture and resets the globe orientation; a late texture callback cannot replace the currently selected planet.

Values are reviewed against the [NASA Venus fact sheet](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/venusfact.html), accessed October 8, 2026. Surface temperature is the **mean** 737 K, rather than invented minimum/maximum values. The 226.6 K black-body temperature is an equilibrium quantity, distinct from the greenhouse-heated surface. The solar day is 2,802 hours (116.75 Earth days), distinct from the retrograde 243-day sidereal rotation and 583.92-day Earth-relative orbital synodic period. Flattening is rounded to zero in the source, rather than established as exactly zero.

Surface pressure is approximately 92 bar (9.2 × 10⁶ Pa), giving 90.7× the retained Earth reference of 101,400 Pa. This is an approximate value, rather than an upper limit. Composition includes the fact sheet's major percentages and minor ppm abundances by volume near the surface. The tooltip distinguishes these gases from sulfuric-acid cloud droplets aloft and notes rounding of major abundances. Magnitudes retain the same definitions as Mercury; −4.8 mag is a brightest value from Earth, rather than a current ephemeris.

Info contains the same six sections as Mercury, with original concise prose and primary sources. Interior uncertainty uses [ESA's EnVision science report](https://sci.esa.int/documents/34375/36249/EnVision_YB_final.pdf); the core's size and phase are not presented as known. The surface section uses [JPL's 2023 Magellan reanalysis](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasas-magellan-data-reveals-volcanic-activity-on-venus/) and separates activity in the 1991 radar interval from present eruptions. Atmospheric appearance uses [NASA's visible/ultraviolet comparison](https://www.nasa.gov/technology/nasa-studies-cubesat-mission-to-solve-venusian-mystery/). Mission paragraphs link to NASA, ESA and JAXA records. Akatsuki's status follows [JAXA's September 18, 2025 termination notice](https://global.jaxa.jp/press/2025/09/20250918-2_e.html), including loss of contact in April 2024. History links to [NASA's Galileo account](https://pwg.gsfc.nasa.gov/stargaze/Svenus.htm) and [JPL's Venus-transit history](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/venus-transit-and-the-search-for-other-worlds/).

## Venus visible-light appearance

The Info hero is an unmodified MESSENGER composite made from visible red, green and blue filters by Gordan Ugarkovic. It approximates natural color, with pale opaque clouds and very little contrast. Its complete original frame and aspect ratio are preserved, including the camera's clipped left edge. Image credit, processing context and license are retained in the linked caption and hover/accessibility metadata. This processed image has a separate **CC BY-NC-ND 3.0** license; it is not claimed as an unrestricted NASA image.

The Specs globe uses [JPL's Mariner 10 visible-frequency cloud texture](https://maps.jpl.nasa.gov/tmaps/venus.html). JPL describes its global extent as cloned from a single observation, so the cloud pattern is illustrative and is not a measured global weather map. The render blends just 4% of that texture with a near-white ivory color in linear space, greatly reducing its supplied golden tint and contrast to approximate the human-eye cloud appearance described by NASA. No radar terrain, ultraviolet cloud bands, surface relief or inferred cloud heights are shown. Roughness is increased to remove Mercury's surface-like highlight and bloom is reduced. Illumination, orientation and drag rotation remain illustrative, with no orbital or weather animation. The globe's tooltip records these limitations without adding visible prose to the strip.

Downloads, exact files, credits and reuse terms are in `src/assets/planets/README.md`.

## Earth specifications and Moon

Earth uses the same dock, Specs/Info controls, globe interaction, temperature tooltips and lock behavior as Mercury and Venus. Reviewed values come from the [NASA Earth fact sheet](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html), last updated November 15, 2024, and match the existing Earth reference quantities. Self-comparisons are omitted. Apparent magnitude explicitly reads `N/A from Earth`: a magnitude seen from another world would require a specified observer, distance and phase. The absolute V magnitude remains the standardized 1 AU, zero-phase value.

The 288 K surface temperature and 101,400 Pa pressure are fixed fact-sheet references, not live climate or weather measurements. Pressure preserves the sheet's 1014 mb rather than substituting the standard atmosphere (101,325 Pa). The composition list gives dry-air volume abundances, with water vapor separately marked variable. Carbon dioxide (420 ppm) and methane (1.94 ppm) retain a visible 2024 qualifier so the source snapshot is not mistaken for a current concentration.

Earth's Moons control opens a compact Moon section with mean radius, mass, surface gravity, orbital semi-major axis, sidereal orbital period and phase-cycle period from the [NASA Moon fact sheet](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/moonfact.html). The orbital size is a center-to-center semi-major axis, not instantaneous distance; the sidereal month (27.3217 days) and synodic phase cycle (29.53 days) are distinct. The heading links to the source. Returning to Mercury or Venus hides the control and clears the section. Orbit remains disabled, consistent with their cards.

## Earth information and imagery

The Info tab has the same six sections as the preceding planet cards, reviewed October 8, 2026. [NASA Earth Facts](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/facts/) supports the introduction, tectonic surface, ocean coverage, magnetic environment and axial-tilt explanation. The interior description uses [USGS seismic evidence](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/interior/index.html) and [USGS's mantle explanation](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/are-tectonic-plates-floating-magma): the mantle is mostly solid rock, not a global magma layer. The Moon's likely impact origin and synchronized rotation follow [NASA Moon Facts](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/facts/).

The orbital text distinguishes the sidereal year, seasonal year, sidereal rotation and mean solar day using the Earth fact sheet. Mission/history labels link to NASA's TIROS, Landsat 1, Terra and DSCOVR/EPIC records, JPL's Eratosthenes educational material, and the Apollo 8 and Apollo 17 image records. Mission dates are historical launch/observation dates; they make no claim about current operational status.

Info displays NASA's published Apollo 17 Blue Marble photograph, AS17-148-22727, taken December 7, 1972, with the full downloaded frame and aspect ratio. The Specs globe instead uses the 2048 × 1024 NASA Blue Marble (2002) global composite. Its tooltip identifies observations from different times and illustrative lighting/orientation; it does not imply current weather. The map includes published topographic shading but supplies no bump, displacement, invented relief or animated clouds. Both original downloaded JPEG files remain unmodified, with full source and credit records in the planet imagery README.
