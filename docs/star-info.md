# Star Info articles

`src/data/star-info.json` holds 15 editorial articles covering the 22 original objects in `src/data/stars.csv`, rewritten and reviewed on 2026-10-26 following the [Info writing guide](info-writing-guide.md). Each introduction leads with what makes the object special; the number of sections scales with the available material, from two for sparsely studied red dwarfs to six for Alpha Centauri. They are original summaries with links to Wikipedia for further reading and to primary sources for recent research. They do not replace catalog measurements or the frozen planet-membership data.

`src/data/nearest-100-info.json` adds 61 text-only articles, rewritten and reviewed on 2026-10-26 following the same guide. Together, the two files cover all 100 non-Sun objects plus the Sun in the nearest-100 catalog with 76 distinct articles. Length follows the material: sparsely documented brown dwarfs and red dwarfs have one section, while well-studied systems such as 61 Cygni, Procyon, Tau Ceti, Gliese 876, 40 Eridani and Altair have four. Data-provenance sections tied to catalog values (UGPS J0722−0540 and WISE 1639−6847 estimates and velocities, van Maanen’s Star progenitor model) are kept unchanged. Wikipedia provides general context, with direct research, observatory, university or NASA links for selected discoveries and contested claims. Articles without a suitable Wikipedia page can use an explicit introduction `source` instead. Each file retains its own review date.

`src/data/constellation-star-info.json` (formerly `bright-star-info.json`) holds the Info articles of the Western constellation stars catalog. Its first 109 articles were written in 2026-10 for the retired bright-stars catalog and rewritten on 2026-10-26 following the [Info writing guide](info-writing-guide.md); on the same date they were re-keyed from retired IDs to live catalog or system IDs, without changing any article content. A further 314 articles, added on 2026-10-26, cover every constellation star brighter than magnitude 4 that previously had no Info: 55 brighter than magnitude 3, from Alsephina and Algieba to Alaybasan, and 259 from magnitude 3 to 4, from Pherkad and Mothallah to Mu Eridani. They were written from the stars’ Wikipedia articles, checked against the source text, with multiple systems keyed by their reviewed system ID. With the shared articles, 445 of the catalog’s 750 objects have Info. Length follows the material, from one section for sparsely documented stars to five for Vega and Betelgeuse. Izar’s article, which documents catalog labels and adopted distances, is unchanged.

The new articles link general background to Wikipedia, with direct research, NASA, ESO and CHARA sources for selected findings. These include Fomalhaut’s collision debris reported in 2025, Vega’s smooth disk, Regulus’s stripped companion, Polaris’s resolved surface, and the July 2026 evidence for Betelgeuse’s likely companion. Disputed planets, uncertain eclipses, possible companions and model interpretations retain their qualifications. These are editorial additions; catalog measurements, astrometry, uncertainties, provenance and component membership are unchanged.

The generated startup availability index covers all three article files. Info fetches only the selected article from `generated/star-articles/`, then caches it for its reviewed components and aliases. Regenerate the individual payloads and index with `npm run catalog:generate` when adding articles. Bright-star focused tests cover every catalog entry, component/alias sharing, startup availability, preserved existing articles, and desktop/mobile card rendering. See [deferred star data](deferred-star-data.md).

`src/star-info.ts` resolves explicit component identities through `starComponentIdentity`. Each multiple system shares one article while selection still refers to the chosen component; articles for reviewed systems are therefore keyed by the system ID, never by a single component’s ID (Aldebaran and Epsilon Scorpii were re-keyed on 2026-10-26 so that their B components share the article). This includes all reviewed components of systems represented in nearest-100, even if a faint companion lies outside its selection cutoff: Gliese 229 A, Ba and Bb share the account of the 2024 close-binary discovery. Other additions include 61 Cygni, Procyon, Epsilon Indi, 40 Eridani, Stein 2051 and the red-dwarf multiples. Reviewed cross-catalog aliases also reuse the article; unreviewed names or nearby positions do not establish identity. Single stars that are not part of a reviewed system can list exact catalog IDs of the same star in an article’s `aliases` field; the generator writes them to `starInfoAliases` in the startup index and leaves them out of the article payloads. Seven aliases, each matched by HIP identifier, give the Western constellation records of Vega, Altair, Arcturus, Pollux, Muphrid, Tau Ceti and Epsilon Eridani the articles written under their nearest-catalog IDs. Objects without articles clear the old content and disable Info.

The selected-object and planet cards share the DOM renderer in `src/object-info-content.ts` and the existing planet Info styles. The original nine illustrated articles retain their locally stored, credited images; all 61 new articles are text-only and add no image assets. See [image provenance](../src/assets/stars/README.md). Captions distinguish photographs, survey composites, infrared or ultraviolet views, and artist's concepts, and distinguish observing dates from publication dates.

Recent research includes the 2025 four-planet Barnard's Star analysis; the 2026 four-planet GJ 887 analysis and additional candidate; September 2026 evidence for patchy water clouds on WISE 0855; the unconfirmed 2025 Alpha Centauri A imaging candidate; the unconfirmed 2024 GJ 65 candidate with ambiguous A/B host; and the 2025 revised mass and orbit of Epsilon Eridani b. Candidate and disputed signals are qualified once in plain language; absences of planets are not stated.

The expansion explicitly distinguishes rejected and candidate planets from established discoveries, including the older claims around Kapteyn’s Star, AD Leonis, Gliese 832, Keid and Gliese 205. The possible unresolved binary 2MASS 0939−2448 remains qualified; its integrated light is not assigned to hypothetical individual components. These are editorial additions only: no catalog measurements, component definitions, provenance or generated catalogs are changed.

Focused coverage is in `src/star-info.test.ts` (all 101 nearest-100 IDs, 76 distinct articles, all reviewed companion identities and aliases, text-only additions and unknown IDs) and `e2e/star-info.spec.ts` (UI availability for every catalog object, existing local-image loading, shared system content including below-cutoff companions, and clearing unsupported articles). Full-suite authorization remains governed by `AGENTS.md`.
