# Star Info articles

`src/data/star-info.json` holds 15 editorial articles covering the 22 original objects in `src/data/stars.csv`, reviewed on 2026-10-11. The articles cover introductions, distinguishing features, history or physical context, and stellar or planetary orbits. They are original summaries with links to Wikipedia for further reading and to primary sources for recent research. They do not replace catalog measurements or the frozen planet-membership data.

`src/data/nearest-100-info.json` adds 61 concise, text-only articles reviewed on 2026-10-15. Together, the two files cover all 100 non-Sun objects plus the Sun in the nearest-100 catalog with 76 distinct articles. Most additions contain a short introduction and two brief sections about discovery, unusual behavior, stellar companions or planetary research; sparsely documented objects have one section. Wikipedia provides general context, with direct research, observatory, university or NASA links for selected discoveries and contested claims. Articles without a suitable Wikipedia page can use an explicit introduction `source` instead. Each file retains its own review date.

`src/data/bright-star-info.json` adds 109 text-only articles reviewed on 2026-10-18. With five existing articles reused, all 126 entries of the bright-stars catalog have Info, representing 114 distinct system articles. Each addition has a short introduction and two brief highlights, with no more than 150 words of body text. The emphasis is on discovery: unusual stellar evolution, rotation, chemistry, pulsation, companions, observing history and cultural context. Multiple systems share one narrative, including reviewed companions below the brightness cutoff. Existing articles and their images are retained.

The new articles link general background to Wikipedia, with direct research, NASA, ESO and CHARA sources for selected findings. These include Fomalhaut’s collision debris reported in 2025, Vega’s smooth disk, Regulus’s stripped companion, Polaris’s resolved surface, and the July 2026 evidence for Betelgeuse’s likely companion. Disputed planets, uncertain eclipses, possible companions and model interpretations retain their qualifications. These are editorial additions; catalog measurements, astrometry, uncertainties, provenance and component membership are unchanged.

The generated startup availability index covers all three article files. Info fetches only the selected article from `generated/star-articles/`, then caches it for its reviewed components and aliases. Regenerate the individual payloads and index with `npm run catalog:generate` when adding articles. Bright-star focused tests cover every catalog entry, component/alias sharing, startup availability, preserved existing articles, and desktop/mobile card rendering. See [deferred star data](deferred-star-data.md).

`src/star-info.ts` resolves explicit component identities through `starComponentIdentity`. Each multiple system shares one article while selection still refers to the chosen component. This includes all reviewed components of systems represented in nearest-100, even if a faint companion lies outside its selection cutoff: Gliese 229 A, Ba and Bb share the account of the 2024 close-binary discovery. Other additions include 61 Cygni, Procyon, Epsilon Indi, 40 Eridani, Stein 2051 and the red-dwarf multiples. Reviewed cross-catalog aliases also reuse the article; unreviewed names or nearby positions do not establish identity. Objects without articles clear the old content and disable Info.

The selected-object and planet cards share the DOM renderer in `src/object-info-content.ts` and the existing planet Info styles. The original nine illustrated articles retain their locally stored, credited images; all 61 new articles are text-only and add no image assets. See [image provenance](../src/assets/stars/README.md). Captions distinguish photographs, survey composites, infrared or ultraviolet views, and artist's concepts, and distinguish observing dates from publication dates.

Recent research includes the 2025 four-planet Barnard's Star analysis; the 2026 four-planet GJ 887 analysis and additional candidate; September 2026 evidence for patchy water clouds on WISE 0855; the unconfirmed 2025 Alpha Centauri A imaging candidate; the unconfirmed 2024 GJ 65 candidate with ambiguous A/B host; and 2025 Webb Epsilon Eridani imaging upper limits. Candidate/disputed signals and habitable-zone uncertainty are retained explicitly. “No confirmed planets” statements refer to the reviewed sources, not proof that a system is planet-free.

The expansion explicitly distinguishes rejected and candidate planets from established discoveries, including the older claims around Kapteyn’s Star, AD Leonis, Gliese 832, Keid and Gliese 205. The possible unresolved binary 2MASS 0939−2448 remains qualified; its integrated light is not assigned to hypothetical individual components. These are editorial additions only: no catalog measurements, component definitions, provenance or generated catalogs are changed.

Focused coverage is in `src/star-info.test.ts` (all 101 nearest-100 IDs, 76 distinct articles, all reviewed companion identities and aliases, text-only additions and unknown IDs) and `e2e/star-info.spec.ts` (UI availability for every catalog object, existing local-image loading, shared system content including below-cutoff companions, and clearing unsupported articles). Full-suite authorization remains governed by `AGENTS.md`.
