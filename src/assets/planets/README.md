# Planet imagery

All files are served locally. Mercury imagery was downloaded October 7, 2026; Venus and Earth imagery October 8, 2026. Original downloaded files are unmodified. Observation, processing, globe-rendering and reuse details are recorded separately below.

## mercury-natural-color.jpg

- **NASA image:** [PIA11364 — Mercury's “True” Color is in the Eye of the Beholder](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/mercurys-true-color-is-in-the-eye-of-the-beholder/).
- **Downloaded file:** [Mercury in true color.jpg, Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mercury_in_true_color.jpg), [original JPEG](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4a/Mercury_in_true_color.jpg), 1040 × 1040 pixels.
- **Credit:** NASA / Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory / Arizona State University / Carnegie Institution of Washington.
- **Acquired:** October 6, 2008, MESSENGER MDIS Wide Angle Camera.
- **Representation:** Commons' existing crop of the bottom-right panel of NASA's four-panel comparison, centered on a black square. The entire disk remains visible. Blue, green and red use 480, 560 and 630 nm filters, with slightly adjusted contrast. This is an approximation of natural color using narrow visible-light filters; it is not the vivid enhanced-color mapping often used to emphasize composition.
- **Reuse:** The file's Commons description marks it public domain as NASA work. [NASA Images and Media Guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/) permit informational use and require acknowledgement of NASA. Full credits and the original source are retained in the app's image link/caption metadata and this file.

- **Credit:** NASA / Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory / Carnegie Institution of Washington / color composite by Gordan Ugarkovic.

The pale, nearly featureless appearance shows opaque clouds rather than exposed surface terrain. It is not a complete continuous-spectrum measurement or an ultraviolet/infrared false-color composite.

## venus-cloud-map.jpg

- **Source:** [NASA/JPL Solar System Simulator: Venus texture maps](https://maps.jpl.nasa.gov/tmaps/venus.html), the final Mariner / fictitious entry.
- **Downloaded file:** [ven0mss2.jpg](https://maps.jpl.nasa.gov/tmaps/pix/ven0mss2.jpg), 1440 × 720 pixels, approximately 45 KB. Downloaded October 8, 2026; stored unmodified.
- **Credit:** David Seal / NASA / JPL-Caltech.
- **Representation:** JPL states that the global map is cloned from a single visible-frequency Mariner 10 image. It is an illustrative repeated cloud texture, not a global observation or current weather map. The globe blends the texture at 4% with a near-white ivory tint in linear color space, reducing the map's golden coloring and strong contrast to approximate Venus's pale, low-contrast visible-light appearance. No bump/displacement map or surface terrain is inferred. The Info hero retains actual visible-color observations and is not derived from this map.
- **Reuse:** JPL's simulator offers the texture for download with creator/owner attribution. Used for informational visualization under [JPL's image-use policy](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/jpl-image-use-policy/) and [NASA Images and Media Guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/), preserving credit, provenance and the illustrative qualification in the globe's tooltip and documentation. No endorsement or logo is used.

- **Source:** [NASA: Apollo 17 Blue Marble](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/apollo-17-blue-marble/), with acquisition metadata in [NASA's AS17-148-22727 record](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/imgcat/html/object_page/a17_h_148_22727.html).
- **Downloaded file:** [NASA's published JPEG scan](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/as17-148-22727_lrg.jpg), 1041 × 1041 pixels, approximately 188 KB. Downloaded October 8, 2026, unmodified. Info preserves its complete published frame and aspect ratio.

- **Acquired:** December 7, 1972, during the translunar journey. Hasselblad color photograph AS17-148-22727.
- **Representation:** A single view showing Africa, Arabia, Madagascar and Antarctica. This is the photographic Blue Marble, distinct from NASA's later satellite-derived global composites. The app makes no additional color adjustments or crops.
- **Reuse:** NASA informational image under [NASA Images and Media Guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/), with source, mission credit and acquisition context retained. No logo or endorsement is used.

## earth-global-map.jpg

- **Source:** [NASA Earth Observatory: The Blue Marble (2002)](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/the-blue-marble-true-color-global-imagery-at-1km-resolution/), the Land Surface, Ocean Color, Sea Ice, and Clouds product (legacy Visible Earth ID 57735).
- **Downloaded file:** [NASA's 2048 × 1024 global JPEG](https://eoimages.gsfc.nasa.gov/images/imagerecords/57000/57735/land_ocean_ice_cloud_2048.jpg), approximately 580 KB. Downloaded October 8, 2026, unmodified; complete 2:1 equirectangular extent.
- **Credit:** NASA / Goddard Space Flight Center. Reto Stöckli (land surface, shallow water, clouds); Robert Simmon (ocean color and compositing), with MODIS teams and supporting NOAA/USGS data.
- **Representation:** Published satellite composite: much of the land/coastal-ocean imagery was collected June–September 2001; clouds combine observations from separate days, including thermal-infrared observations over the poles. The source contains topographic shading and processed ocean color. It approximates a visible-color global appearance rather than a single instant or purely visible-light exposure. The globe maps the supplied colors directly with soft illustrative lighting, high roughness and restrained bloom. Clouds are fixed in the map; no relief, atmospheric height or current weather is inferred.
- **Reuse:** NASA describes the Blue Marble products as freely available to educators, scientists, museums and the public. Used under [NASA Images and Media Guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/), retaining image credits, provenance and the composite qualification in documentation and the globe tooltip.
