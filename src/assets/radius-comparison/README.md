# Radius comparison planetary imagery

These photographic maps are loaded locally, only when their planet is used as a comparison reference. The renderer projects them orthographically onto an oblate spheroid at the same physical scale as the selected star. No external request is made by the application.

- `jupiter-map.png`: 3600 × 1800 full-resolution [Hubble Jupiter global map (2019)](https://science.nasa.gov/asset/hubble/jupiter-global-map-2019/). [Original download](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/missions/hubble/releases/2019/08/STScI-01EVSV9A3VN7VYXN5H6Z1GDG93.tif/jcr:content/renditions/Full%20Res.png). Credit: NASA, ESA, A. Simon (Goddard Space Flight Center), and M.H. Wong (University of California, Berkeley). The photographic map excludes latitudes above 80 degrees; the renderer extends the closest observed row over the small unobserved caps. The display meridian places the Great Red Spot slightly left of center. This is a recognizable fixed reference view, not a live view of Jupiter.
- `earth-map.png`: 2048 × 1024 [NASA Blue Marble satellite mosaic](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/2915/). [Original download](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a000000/a002900/a002915/bluemarble-2048.png). Credit: NASA/Goddard Space Flight Center Scientific Visualization Studio; Blue Marble data courtesy of NASA's Earth Observatory and Reto Stockli (NASA/GSFC). The display shows Africa and Europe, rather than the current terrestrial rotation and illumination.

- `earth-clouds.jpg`: 2048 × 1024 NASA Blue Marble cloud composite, retained unmodified from the [original download](https://eoimages.gsfc.nasa.gov/images/imagerecords/57000/57747/cloud_combined_2048.jpg). Credit: NASA Goddard Space Flight Center; Reto Stöckli, with enhancements by Robert Simmon. [NASA describes the cloud mosaic and compositing](https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/earth-matters/2011/10/06/crafting-the-blue-marble/). Brightness supplies the opacity over the land/ocean map. This historical composite is not current weather.

The planetary shading uses slightly off-axis daylight, soft grazing-angle (Fresnel-like) haze, and a thin atmospheric rim. Earth uses a deep-blue ocean hue and a blue rim; Jupiter retains sharp photographic bands, with pale inner-limb haze that follows the daylight, a feathered edge, and a faint optical blur confined to the silhouette. The treatment is visually guided by the [Hubble 2019 portrait](https://science.nasa.gov/asset/hubble/jupiter-2019/) and [Cassini true-color PIA04866 mosaic](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/images/pia04866-cassini-jupiter-portrait/); it is an illustrative approximation, not a fitted atmospheric model. These illustrative lighting effects do not change either body's geometric radius.

Usage policies: [NASA media usage](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/) and [ESA/Hubble image usage](https://esahubble.org/copyright/). Credits and source links are also shown in the card's expanded imagery details.
